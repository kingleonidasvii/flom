About

Dr. Nancy Mykoff is a historian of the United States. She has taught American History and Gender Studies at University College Roosevelt, the international honors college of Utrecht University in Middelburg, the Netherlands, since 1997.

Her passions are teaching and learning about, and with, Native American and Canadian histories and lives, as well as race, gender and media studies.

A recent grant from CHARM-EU supports her current project, Innovating Education. It explores how Indigenous and Western ways of knowing can strengthen each other through cross-cultural collaborations built on trust, reciprocity and equity. The project uses experiential learning, including the arts, to share and create new knowledge and communities that bridge social divides.

She is also the founder and leader of The Walking With Pride and Power Project, a traveling collaborative textile artwork that gives voice to queer and marginalized people.

She has supervised theses at universities across the Netherlands and serves on the board of the Netherlands American Studies Association.

Projects

Current projects

  • Innovating EducationCHARM-EU Grant, 2026–2028. A cross-cultural project exploring how Western and Indigenous ways of knowing meet, focusing on people marginalized by gender, race and age. It includes transnational art-activism such as Photo/Voice and The Walking With Pride and Power Project.
  • Environmental StoriesJoint Student & Faculty Research Grant, University College Roosevelt, 2026. Part of TREnD (Teaching Resilience and Environmental Democracy), an Erasmus+ project. It builds interactive living maps centered on Indigenous stories about landscapes, waterways and ecosystems, documenting places of cultural, ecological and historical significance through 360-degree photography, storytelling, artwork and multimedia.
  • The Walking With Pride and Power ProjectFounder and leader. A collaborative, transnational artwork against the persecution of queer and marginalized people. Participants each braid a piece of a shared rag rug from fabrics that mean something to them and attach their own words. The rug travels to exhibition spaces internationally.

Recent projects

  • IntoleranceVirtual International Samenwerking (VIS) Grant, Dutch Ministry of Education, Culture and Science, 2024–2025. A transnational exploration of the dis/empowerment of queer people in the Netherlands and the United States amid the global shift to the political right, using arts-based research and learning.
  • Stolen Lives: The Indian Residential SchoolUtrecht University Community Engaged Learning Grant, 2023–2024. A creative collaboration with Algoma University, housed in the former Shingwauk Residential School, contributing images, poetry, prose and stories to the Stolen Lives exhibition in Sault Ste. Marie, Canada.
  • De/Colonization: The Legacy of the Indian Boarding School in the U.S. and CanadaVIS Grant, Dutch Ministry of Education, Culture and Science, 2023–2024. Resulted in the Stolen Lives exhibition on survivors of the Shingwauk Residential School and its website.
  • Going Glocal: The Presence of the Past in Native America2022–2023. A two-semester community engaged learning course on how de/colonization plays out in daily life on the Standing Rock Sioux Reservation, combining classroom learning in the Netherlands with experiential learning in North and South Dakota.
